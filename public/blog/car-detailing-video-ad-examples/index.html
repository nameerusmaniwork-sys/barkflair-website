Car detailing video ad examples (with real results)

Six real car detailing video ads we edited for a mobile detailer, why each one works, what to copy for your own ads, and the results behind them.

The fastest way to make a car detailing ad that books jobs is to study ones that already worked. Below are six real video ads we edited for Blue Ridge Mobile Detailing, a mobile detailer, with what each one does, why it works and what you can copy for your own ads.

The results behind these ads

Between January and July 2026, while running our video ads and website, Blue Ridge grew from about $4,000 to $54,000 in monthly sales. One offer-led Meta ad test alone brought in 16 leads and 4 sales from $23.37 in ad spend.

Monthly sales figures were shared by the owner and cover the whole business, which also grew through the owner’s own work and other factors. Every business, offer and budget is different, so results are not guaranteed.

1. The price-on-screen offer ad

What it does: A $199 mobile detailing special. The offer, "Mobile detailing special $199, we come to you", stays pinned at the top for the whole ad while fast interior clips play underneath: carpet extraction, floor mats, a drill brush on the seats. It ends on "Book your mobile detail starting at $199" and the logo.

Why it works: Anyone who watches even two seconds knows the price and that you come to them. That filters out price shoppers before they message you, so the leads you get are ready to book.

Copy this: Pin your offer and price as a text bar for the full length of the ad, not just at the end.

2. The seasonal special

What it does: A $279 detail, clay and ceramic package labeled "August Special". The process shots build from interior cleaning to a split-screen hood: one half dull, one half glossy.

Why it works: A month in the offer gives people a reason to book now instead of "someday". The split-screen before-and-after proves the result in one frame.

Copy this: Name the month or season in your offer, and end on a split-screen before-and-after of the same panel.

3. The problem-first ceramic coating ad

What it does: A $399 mobile ceramic coating ad with voiceover. It opens on the problem, "rock chips, swirl marks, UV rays, harsh weather", then speaks to new and luxury car owners, explains that the coating is installed at home or work, and ends with a call to book a free inspection.

Why it works: Ceramic coating is a higher-ticket service, so the ad sells the reason before the product. Naming the exact damage makes owners of newer cars picture their own paint.

Copy this: For premium services, open on the problem your customer is afraid of, then show the fix and the convenience.

4. The step-by-step process ad

What it does: A ceramic coating and paint correction ad on a classic car, with a bold label on every step: Pro ceramic coating, Paint correction, Deep foam wash, Hand wash & prep, Mirror finish. It finishes on the branded van and the logo.

Why it works: Labels turn satisfying footage into education. Viewers learn what a proper detail includes, which makes the price feel earned.

Copy this: Put a two- or three-word label on each step of your process so the ad works with the sound off.

5. The brand awareness ad

What it does: A voiceover ad that lists everything the business details, "daily drivers and family vehicles to motorcycles, boats, RVs, commercial fleets", plus the service area and the fact that they run multiple mobile units.

Why it works: It is not trying to sell one package. It builds trust and shows scale, which makes the offer ads above convert better when the same people see them later.

Copy this: Run one brand ad alongside your offer ads so people who see your offers already know who you are.

6. The audience call-out ad

What it does: A Ford F-150 truck detailing ad that opens with "Your truck works hard. Getting it detailed shouldn’t." It covers the convenience (no water or power needed, done in your driveway) and ends with "Book online in 60 seconds".

Why it works: Calling out one type of customer in the first line makes truck owners stop scrolling, because the ad is clearly about them.

Copy this: Make one version of your ad for each type of customer you want: trucks, family SUVs, new cars, classics.

What all six ads have in common

  • The offer or the customer is named in the first seconds, never a slow logo intro.
  • A clear price on the offer ads, so the wrong customers filter themselves out.
  • "We come to you" is said or shown, because convenience is what sells mobile detailing.
  • Short, satisfying process clips of foam, extraction, polishing and the final shine.
  • One call to action at the end: book, get an inspection or book online.

Make your own detailing ad

  1. Pick one offer with a price, such as an interior detail or a ceramic coating package.
  2. Film a dirty before shot, 5 to 8 short process clips and a slow reveal of the finished car.
  3. Write three different opening lines: one with the price, one calling out a customer type and one naming a problem.
  4. Run the three versions with the same budget and keep the one with the lowest cost per lead.
